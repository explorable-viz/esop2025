\section{Surface Language}
\label{sec:surfacesemantics}
When presenting a program execution, we want to give the appearence to the user that they are executing parts
of the surface language directly. To that end we formalise and present a notion of evaluation of surface
terms, which we present to the user in the debugger. We most formalise a notion of values in the surface
language, which mostly correspond to values in the core language. We then present proof that directly
evaluating a surface program produces the same result as desugaring it to a core program and then evaluating, justifying our presentation of the surface semantics to the debugger.

\subsection{Compatible Patterns}
One complication of defining semantics for the surface language is how to deal with functions defined
equationally. In particular, functions defined with equations are subject to conditions on how they perform
pattern matching. We need to define a notion of pattern matching for surface terms, that roughly corresponds
to pattern matching in the core language. This is made more complicated by the lack of eliminators in the
surface language. We therefore need to define a notion of compatible patterns for defining functions
equationally, and a notion of pattern matching for surface values.

\subsection{Desugaring Of Values, Environments and RecDefs}
In order to state the theorem, we must extend our definition of desugaring to values. For values, we only have
one interesting case, that of surface closures. To define the desugaring of surface-closures, we need to define
desugaring for environments, and recursively-defined functions (in surface language $\seq{g}$, in core $\uprho$).
Once this extension to the desugaring relation has been carefully defined, in figure \ref{fig:closures}, we can state and prove our theorem.

\subsection{Equivalence of Semantics}
Informally, the equivalence of the semantics means that desugaring a surface term and surface environment
produces the same result as surface-evaluating to a surface value and then desugaring the surface value. We
need this theorem, as it is our intent that the user is presented with the surface semantics, even though we
execute the program using the core semantics. Without this theorem, we could not properly justify this choice,
as fluid has some non-trivial desugaring. We formalise the equivalence of these semantics as the following
theorem.
\begin{theorem}
   \label{theorem:semantics}
   Suppose $\Gamma \vdash s : A, \; \upgamma : \Gamma, \; s \desugarFwdS e, \; and \; \upgamma \desugarFwdS \upgamma'$, then:
\hfill
\begin{enumerate}
   \item $ If \; \upgamma, s \evalSugS v \; then \; \upgamma', e \evalFwdS v' \; where \; v \desugarFwdS v'$
   \item $If \; \upgamma',e \evalFwdS v' \; then \; \upgamma, s \evalSugS v \; where \; v \desugarFwdS v'$
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
Cases of theorem \ref{theorem:semantics} rely on pattern-matching in the surface and core languages producing
environments that coincide. We state this as lemma \ref{lemma:matching-bi}. For the variable case, we need to
also establish that environment lookup commutes with desugaring, lemma \ref{lemma:varlookup}. Due to the fact
that desugaring of values is not a total relation, we require that both $s$ and $\upgamma$ can be desugared as
the precondition to the first case of theorem \ref{theorem:semantics}.
This will be properly explained in the final version of the paper.

\begin{lemma}
   \label{lemma:varlookup}
   Suppose we have $\upgamma \desugarFwdS \upgamma'$ and $v \desugarFwdS v'$, then
   $$
      \bind{x}{v} \in \upgamma \quad \iff \quad \bind{x}{v'} \in \upgamma'
   $$
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
   This follows from the definition of $\upgamma \desugarFwdS \upgamma'$. If $\upgamma := \set{\seq{\bind{y}{v}}}$ and
   $\upgamma' := \set{\seq{\bind{y'}{v'}}}$ then $\bind{x}{v} \in \upgamma \implies \exists i \le \length{\upgamma}. \, \exVar{x} = y_i$ and $v = v_i$.
   Then by definition of $\upgamma \desugarFwdS \upgamma'$, we have that $u_i \desugarFwdS {u_i}' = v'$, and so we have that $\bind{x}{v'} \in \upgamma'$.
   Going backwards, $\bind{x}{v'} \in \upgamma' \implies \exists i \le \length{\upgamma'}. \, \exVar{x} = y_i$ and $v = {v_i}'$. By definition of
   $\upgamma \desugarFwdS \upgamma'$, $\exists v_i.\, v_i \desugarFwdS {v_i}'$ where $\bind{y_i}{v_i} \in \upgamma$, which means $\bind{x}{v} \in \upgamma$.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}
   \label{lemma:matching-bi}
   Suppose we have $v \desugarFwdS v'$, and $\upmu \desugarFwdS \upsigma$, Then:
   $$
      v, \upmu \matchFwdS \upgamma, \; where \; \upgamma \desugarFwdS \upgamma' \quad \iff \quad \exists \upkappa.\, v',\upsigma \matchFwdS \upgamma', \upkappa \; where \; \upgamma \desugarFwdS \upgamma'
   $$
\end{lemma}
