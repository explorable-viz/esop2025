\begin{abstract}
   Galois connections between Boolean algebras offer an appealing formal framework for bidirectional dynamic
   program slicing, characterising fine-grained input-output relationships in terms of demand and sufficiency.
   \citeauthor{perera22} showed how the De Morgan dual of a program slicing algorithm can be used to pick out
   \emph{competing outputs}, namely the other outputs that are related to some outputs of interest in the
   sense of demanding some of the same inputs. However implementations to date of this ``Galois slicing''
   approach have been too slow for interactive applications because they have relied on rewinding or replaying
   the entire computation using a sequential execution record, or \emph{trace}.

   In this paper we present a new implementation technique for Galois slicing based on dynamic dependence
   graphs, an approach used in the past for analysing imperative programs. We decompose the analysis into two
   steps: an operational semantics that evaluates a program to a dependence graph, and then a pair of adjoint
   operators for forwards and backwards slicing over the graph. The overhead of producing a dependence graph
   is comparable to that of producing a trace, but the slicing algorithms run on average much faster than
   their trace-based counterparts, making them suitable for interactive use. We also show that, extensionally,
   forward slicing over a dependence graph is the De Morgan dual of backward slicing over the opposite graph,
   leading to an easy implementation of the upper adjoint in terms of the lower. Even this naive
   implementation is on average faster than the trace-based approach, but we also give a even faster direct
   implementation. The slicing algorithms are language-agnostic, and the mathematical setup is simpler too:
   the Galois connections are determined solely by the reachability relation $R$ between the inputs and
   outputs of the graph. The competing outputs operator from earlier work is given by the Galois connection
   for $R \relcomp R^{-1}$, and a new \emph{cooperating inputs} operator is given by $R^{-1} \relcomp R$. We
   show how this new operator also has useful data visualisation applications.
\end{abstract}
