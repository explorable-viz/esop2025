\begin{abstract}
   Galois connections between Boolean algebras offer an appealing formal framework for bidirectional dynamic
   program slicing, characterising fine-grained input-output relationships in terms of demand and sufficiency.
   \citeauthor{perera22} showed how the De Morgan dual of a program slicing algorithm can be used to pick out
   \emph{related outputs}, namely the outputs that ``compete'' with a given set of outputs in the sense of
   demanding some of the same inputs. However implementations to date of this ``Galois slicing'' approach have
   been too slow for interactive applications because they have relied on rewinding or replaying the entire
   computation using a sequential execution record, or \emph{trace}.

   In this paper we present a new implementation technique for Galois slicing based on dynamic dependence
   graphs, an approach used in the program slicing literature for analysing imperative programs. Our approach
   decomposes Galois slicing into two components: an operational semantics that evaluates a program to a
   dependence graph, and a pair of adjoint operators for forwards and backwards slicing over the graph. We
   show that although the overhead of producing a dependence graph is comparable to that of producing a trace,
   the slicing algorithms run on average much faster than their trace-based counterparts, making them suitable
   for interactive use. We also show that, extensionally, forward slicing over a graph is simply the De Morgan
   dual of backward slicing over the opposite graph, leading to an easy implementation of the upper adjoint in
   terms of the lower. Even this naive implementation is on average faster than the trace-based approach, but
   we also give a direct implementation of forward slicing over a graph and show that it performs better
   still. We also compare the graphical approach qualitatively, and conclude that the two-phase approach (graph-building semantics + analyses over the graph) is also much simpler to implement.
