\section{Conjugate Operators For Dynamic Dependence Graphs}
\label{sec:conjugate}

In this section we introduce Boolean algebras and other preliminaries (\secref{conjugate:notation}); dynamic
dependence graphs (\secref{conjugate:dependence-graphs}), and the image and preimage functions for a relation
and their De Morgan duals (\secref{conjugate:image-preimage}). We then extend these operators to dependence
graphs (\secref{conjugate:dependence-graph-functions}) via their IO relation, and give algorithms for computing
them over a given graph (\secref{conjugate:dependence-graph-algos}).

\subsection{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:conjugate:notation}
We give some notation used in the rest of the paper. $X, Y$ range over finite sets.

\paragraph{Finite sequences}
Write to ${\seq{x}}$ denote a finite sequence of elements $\seqRange{x_1}{x_n}$, with $\seqEmpty$ as the empty sequence. Concatenation of two sequences $\seq{x}$ and $\seq{x}'$ is written $\seq{x} \concat \seq{x}'$.
We also write $x \cons \seq{x}$ for cons (prepend) and $\seq{x} \cons x$ for snoc (append).

\paragraph{Finite maps}
Write $\set{\seq{\bind{k}{x}}}$ to denote a set of pairs $\seqRange{\bind{k_1}{x_1}}{\bind{k_n}{x_n}}$ where
keys $k_i$ are pairwise unique.

\paragraph{Disjoint union}
Write $X \disjunion Y$ to mean $X \cup Y$ where $X$ and $Y$ are disjoint, and also write $x \cdot X$ or $X \cdot x$ to mean $X \disjunion \set{x}$. For finite maps $\phi, \psi$ write $\phi \disjunion \psi$ to mean $\phi \cup \psi$ where $\dom{\phi}$ and $\dom{\psi}$ are disjoint.

\paragraph{Relative complement}
Write $X \setminus Y$ for the relative complement of $Y$ in $X$, i.e.~the set of elements of
$X$ that are not in $Y$.

\paragraph{Relational converse}
For a relation $R \subseteq X \times Y$, write $R^{-1} \subseteq Y \times X$ to denote the \emph{converse}
relation where $(y,x) \in R^{-1} \iff (x,y) \in R$.

\subsubsection{Boolean algebras}

A \emph{Boolean algebra} (or Boolean lattice) is a 6-tuple $A = (A, \meet, \join, \bot, \top, \neg)$ with
carrier $A$, distinguished elements bottom ($\bot$) and top ($\top$), commutative operations meet ($\meet$)
and join ($\join$) with $\top$ and $\bot$ as respective units, and a unary operation negate ($\neg$)
satisfying $x \join \neg x = \top$ and $x \meet \neg x = \bot$. The operations $\meet$ and $\join$ distribute
over each other and induce a partial order $\leq$ over $X$.

Often we will be considering a specific finite Boolean algebra, namely the powerset $\powerset{X}$ whose
carrier is the set of subsets $X' \subseteq X$ ordered by inclusion $\subseteq$. Bottom is the empty set
$\emptyset$, top is the whole set $X$, meet and join are given by $\cap$ and $\cup$ and negation by relative
complement, so that $\neg X' = X \setminus X'$.

\subsection{Dynamic Dependence Graphs}
\label{sec:conjugate:dependence-graphs}

We explain how we arrive at specific dependence graphs in \secref{core}, and here just introduce the basic
notion. A \emph{dynamic dependence graph}~\cite{agrawal90} is a directed acyclic graph $G = (V,E)$ with a set
$V$ of \emph{vertices} and set $E \subseteq V \times V$ of edges. In the dependence graph for a particular
program, vertices $\alpha, \beta \in V$ represents parts of values (either supplied to the program as inputs
or produced during evaluation) and edges $(\alpha, \beta) \in E$ indicate that, in the evaluation of the
program, the partial value associated to $\beta$ \textit{depends on} on the partial value associated to
$\alpha$. This diverges somewhat from traditional approaches to dynamic dependence graphs in only considering
one type of edge (data dependency) rather than separate data and control dependencies; moreover our edges
point in the direction of dependent vertices. (In the literature the other direction is somewhat more common.)

\begin{definition}[In-edges and out-edges]
For a graph $G=(V,E)$ and vertex $\alpha \in V$, write $\inE{G}(\alpha)$ for the in-edges of $\alpha$ in $G$
and $\outE{G}(\alpha)$ for its out-edges.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Sources and sinks]
For a graph $G = (V,E)$, write $\sources{G}$ for the \emph{sources} of $G$ (i.e.~those vertices with no
in-edges) and $\sinks{G}$ for the \emph{sinks} of $G$ (those with no out-edges).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Reachability relation]
Define the \emph{reachability relation} for a graph $G=(V,E)$ to be the reflexive transitive closure of $E$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Opposite graph]
For a graph $G$ define the \emph{opposite} graph $\opGraph{G} \eqdef (V,E^{-1})$ where $\param^{-1}$ denotes
relational converse.
\end{definition}

\noindent (Taking the opposite graph has the effect of swapping sources and sinks.)

\subsubsection{IO Relation for a Graph}

Reachability in $G$ induces a relation just between sources and sinks, which we call the \emph{IO} relation of
$G$.

\begin{definition}[IO relation]
For any dependence graph $G$ with reachability relation $R$, define the \emph{IO} relation of $G$ to be $R \cap (\sources{G} \times \sinks{G})$.
\end{definition}

The IO relation specifies how specific inputs (sources) are demanded by specific outputs (sinks). (Thus we
will interpret $(x, y) \in R$ as ``$x$ is \textit{demanded by} $y$''.) Since we are in fact interested in the
relationship between input and outputs \emph{selections} (namely sets of inputs and outputs), we now consider
how such a relation $R \subseteq X \times Y$ lifts to $\powerset{X}$ and $\powerset{Y}$ via the \textit{image}
and \textit{preimage} functions for $R$, which we will denote using $\demandedByR$ and $\demandsR$.

\subsection{Conjugate Image and Preimage Functions}
\label{sec:conjugate:image-preimage}

\begin{definition}[Image and Preimage Functions for a Relation]
   For a relation $R \subseteq X \times Y$, define $\demandedByR_R: \powerset{X} \to \powerset{Y}$ and
   $\demandsR_R : \powerset{Y} \to \powerset{X}$ as:
   \begin{enumerate}
      \item $\demandedByR_{R}(X') \eqdef \set{y \in Y \mid \exists x \in X'.(x,y) \in R}$ \hfill (image)
      \item $\demandsR_{R}(Y') \eqdef \set{x \in X \mid \exists y \in Y'. (x,y) \in R}$ \hfill (preimage)
   \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\noindent Clearly the image function for $R$ is the preimage function for its converse, i.e.~$\demandedByR_{R}
= \demandsR_{R^{-1}}$. When thinking of $R$ as an IO relation, the image $\demandedByR_{R}(X')$ consists of
all outputs that any element of $X'$ is demanded by, and the preimage $\demandsR_{R}(Y')$ consists of all
inputs demanded by any element of $Y'$. If $R$ happens to be a function, the image and preimage functions form
a \emph{Galois connection} (\defref{conjugate:galois-connection} below); for a proper relation they form a
\textit{conjugate pair}, in the sense of \citet{jonsson51}:

%Notably, the set of outputs that a set of inputs is necessary for is a superset of the outputs it is
%sufficient for.

\begin{definition}[Conjugate Functions]
   For Boolean algebras $A$, $B$, functions $f: A \to B$ and $g: B \to A$ form a \textit{conjugate pair} iff:
   $$
   f(x) \meet y = \bot \iff x \meet g(y) = \bot
   $$
\end{definition}

\noindent (\citeauthor{jonsson51} consider only endofunctions; here we extend the idea of conjugacy to maps
between Boolean algebras.)

\begin{lemma}[Preimage and Image of a relation are conjugate]
For any relation $R \subseteq X \times Y$ the functions $\demandedByR_{R}$ and $\demandsR_{R}$ are conjugate.
\end{lemma}

This should be intuitive enough: for any subsets $X' \subseteq X$ and $Y' \subseteq Y$, if the elements ``on
the right'' to which $X'$ is related are disjoint from $Y'$, then there are no edges in $R$ from $X'$ to $Y'$;
and in virtue of that, the elements ``on the left'' to which $Y'$ is related must also be disjoint from $X'$.

\subsubsection{Dual Image and Preimage Functions}

If $R$ is an IO relation, then $\demandedByR_R(X)$ effectively asks ``what is $X$ necessary for?'' This
naturally leads to the question ``what is $X$ \emph{sufficient} for?'' In fact, we can use the notion of
sufficiency as a test of correctness. If we begin by selecting an output of interest, we would expect the data
it demands to also be sufficient to reconstruct it. This leads us to define the following pair of functions,
which also form a conjugate pair.

\begin{definition}[Dual Image and Preimage Functions for a Relation]
   For any relation $R \subseteq X \times Y$, define $\sufficesR_R: \powerset{X} \to \powerset{Y}$ and $\preimageDualR_R: \powerset{Y} \to \powerset{X}$ as:
   \begin{enumerate}
      \item $\sufficesR_R(X') \eqdef \set{y \in Y \mid \nexists x \in X \setminus X'.(x, y) \in R}$ \hfill (dual image)
      \item $\preimageDualR_R(Y') \eqdef \set{x \in X \mid \nexists y \in Y \setminus Y'.(x, y) \in R}$ \hfill (dual preimage)
   \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

This definition is similar to $\demandedByR_R$ except that $\sufficesR_R$ needs every ancestor to be in the
input set, rather than any ancestor to be in the input set. We can, in fact, turn this query on sufficiency
into $\demandedByR_R$, using the De Morgan Dual.

\begin{definition}[De Morgan Dual]
   Suppose a function $f: A \to B$ between Boolean algebras. Define the \textit{De Morgan Dual} $\dual{f}: A
   \to B$ of $f$ as:
   \[ \dual{f} \eqdef \neg_B \after f \after \neg_A \]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Galois connection]
\label{def:conjugate:galois-connection}
Suppose $A, B$ are partial orders. Then monotone functions $f: A \to B$ and $g: B \to A$ form a \textit{Galois
connection} iff
\[ f(x) \leq y \iff x \leq g(y) \]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Correspondence Between Conjugates and Galois Connections]
   Suppose functions $f: A \to B$ and $g: B \to A$ between Boolean algebras. The following statements are
   equivalent:
   \begin{enumerate}
      \item $f$ and $g$ form a conjugate pair
      \item $f$ and $\dual{g}$ form a Galois connection
   \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[Duality of image and preimage functions]
   \item
   \begin{enumerate}
      \item $\dual{\demandedByR_R} = \sufficesR_R$
      \item $\dual{\demandsR_R} = \preimageDualR_R$
   \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

We observe that if we restrict the set of inputs $X$ and outputs $Y$ in the graph such that there are no
inputs which are unused, then $\demandsR$ will preserve $\bot$, i.e.~$\demandsR(\emptyset) = \emptyset$. This
means that applying $\demandedByR$ after $\demandsR$, we will not lose any of the originally selected inputs.

\begin{lemma}
\item
\begin{enumerate}
   \item If $\demandedByR_R(\emptyset) = \emptyset$, then $\demandsR_R \after \demandedByR_R$ is inflationary.
   \item If $\demandsR_R(\emptyset) = \emptyset$, then $\demandedByR_R \after \demandsR_R$ is inflationary.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

Similarly, if we have no outputs which are free, then $\demandedByR$ will also preserve $\bot$, and composing
the functions in the opposite way will also be increasing.

As an easy corollary, if $\demandsR$ and $\demandedByR$ both preserve $\emptyset$ then they do form a Galois
connection, albeit an antitone one. This is useful to know, since we may be working in a context where we wish
to consider a filtered view of the data, where the user is shown only the data used by the original program.

\subsection{$\demandsR$ and $\demandedByR$ over Dependence Graphs}
\label{sec:conjugate:dependence-graph-functions}

We now extend the notion of $\demandsR$ and $\demandedByR$ to a dependence graph $G$ via its IO
relation. The set of outputs \emph{demanded by} some inputs $X$ is simply the subset of the outputs of $G$
reachable from $X$, i.e.~the intersection of $\demandedByR_{R}(X)$ with $\sinks{G}$. Conversely, the set of
inputs that some outputs $Y$ \emph{demands} is simply the subset of the inputs of $G$ reachable from $Y$ by
traversing the graph in the opposite direction, i.e.~the intersection of $\demandsR_{R}$ with $\sources{G}$.

\begin{definition}[$\demandsR$ and $\demandedByR$ for a dependence graph]
   For a graph $G$ with IO relation $R$, define:
   \begin{enumerate}
      \item $\demandedByR_G \eqdef \demandedByR_{R}: \powerset{\sources{G}} \to \powerset{\sinks{G}}$ \hfill (demanded by)
      \item $\demandsR_G \eqdef \demandsR_{R}: \powerset{\sinks{G}} \to \powerset{\sources{G}}$ \hfill (demands)
   \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Now we can provide a formal account of the notions of related inputs and related outputs introduced in
\secref{overview}.

\begin{definition}[Related Inputs and Outputs]
   For a dependence graph $G$ define:
   \begin{enumerate}
      \item $\relInputR_{G} \eqdef \demandsR_{G} \after \demandedByR_{G}$\hfill (related inputs)
      \item $\relOutputR_{G} \eqdef \demandedByR_{G} \after \demandsR_{G}$\hfill (related outputs)
   \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Intuitively, related outputs and related inputs are relations of \emph{cognacy} (common ancestry) in $G$ and
$\opGraph{G}$ respectively. For a set of inputs $X \subseteq \sources{G}$, related inputs asks ``what other
inputs are demanded by the outputs which demand X?'', and first finds all outputs $\demandedByR_{G}(X)$ that
our inputs are demanded by, and then computes the inputs that those outputs demand,
i.e.~$\demandsR_{G}(\demandedByR_{G}(X'))$. For a set of outputs $Y \subseteq \sinks{G}$, related outputs asks
``what other outputs demand the inputs that Y demands'', and first finds all inputs $\demandsR_{G}(Y')$ that
our outputs demand, and then computes the outputs that those inputs $\demandsR_{G}(Y)$ demand,
i.e.~$\demandedByR_{G}(\demandsR_{G}(Y))$.

\subsection{Computing $\demandsR_G$ and $\demandedByR_G$}
\label{sec:conjugate:dependence-graph-algos}

We now show how to compute $\demandedByR_{G}$ and $\demandsR_{G}$ for a dependence graph $G$. An alternative
presentation of graphs in terms of adjacency maps is useful notationally.

\begin{definition}[Graph as adjacency map]
   A graph $G = (V,E)$ can also be specified as an \emph{adjacency map} $G: V \to \powerset{V}$ from vertices
   to sets of out neighbours, where
   \[G(\alpha) = V' \iff \alpha \in V\text{ and }\forall\beta \in V'.(\alpha, \beta) \in E\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[$\demandedByAlg{}$]
\figref{conjugate:algorithms:demands} defines the family of relations $\demandedByAlg{}$.
\end{definition}

For a dependence graph $G_0$ and inputs $X \subseteq \sources{G_0}$, the judgement $X\;
\smash{\demandedByAlg{G_0}} Y$ says that $X$ is demanded by $Y \subseteq \sinks{G_0}$. It makes use of an
auxiliary definition $\demandedByV{G_0}$ which maintains a set of vertices still to process and builds the
subgraph $G \subseteq G_0$ consisting of all vertices reachable from the initial set and all edges between
them. If $X$ is empty (\ruleName{done}) the reachable subgraph $G$ is complete and can be returned. Otherwise
we select an arbitrary vertex $\alpha$ from $X$. If $\alpha$ is already in $G$ (\ruleName{skip}), there is
nothing to do at this step and we continue with the remaining vertices. Otherwise, we add the out-star at
$\alpha$ to $G$ and the out-neighbours to the pending vertices (\ruleName{extend}). When $\demandedByV{}$ is
done, $\demandedByAlg{}$ extracts the output set from the accumulator by intersecting with $\sinks{G}$.

\begin{proposition}[$\demandedByAlg{}$ Computes $\demandedByR$]
   For any dependence graph $G$, any $X \subseteq \sources{G}$ and any $Y \subseteq \sinks{G}$ we have
   \[X \demandedByAlg{G} Y \iff \demandedByR_G(X) = Y\]
\end{proposition}

We can also compute $\demandedByR$ using the De Morgan dual of $\sufficesR$. This is potentially more
efficient in situations where less graph is traversed than it would be otherwise. We therefore provide a more
efficient implementation of the sufficiency analysis, detailed in \figref{conjugate:slicing:forward}. Our
implementation makes it easy to flip between $G$ and $\opGraph{G}$ so we free make use of both in the
algorithm. Note, it is also possible to read an algorithm directly from the inductive
definition of the $\demandedByAlg{}$ relation since it is actually a function
with its left-most argument $V$ and index $G_0$ as inputs.\footnote{Any choice between \ruleName{skip} and \ruleName{extend} is
made deterministic by the disjointness of $G$ and $H$ in \ruleName{extend} implying the opposite
side condition to \ruleName{skip} ($\alpha \not\in \V(G)$), and with termination implied by eventual
saturation of the first argument (left-most) by all of the edges in the indexing graph
leading from the vertices in second argument, after
which a finite sequence of \ruleName{skip} can be applied to reach the base case of \ruleName{done}.}

For a dependence graph $G_0$, the algorithm $\sufficesAlg{}$ also delegates to an auxiliary procedure
$\sufficesE{}$ which builds a subgraph of $G_0$, similar to $\demandsAlg{}$. The judgement $\sufficesE{}$
takes the subgraph under construction $G$, a ``pending'' subgraph $H \subseteq G_0$ with $\sources{H}
\subseteq \sinks{G}$, and the current set of edges under consideration $E$. For efficiency, the algorithm
traverses each edge in the graph at most once.

\figref{suffices-algo-diagram} shows an example run of the algorithm. Green indicates included into $G$
because we have found sufficient inputs for them; blue vertices are unvisited; orange vertices are ``pending''
in $H$ with at least one of their in-edges from $G_0$. Dashed edges indicate the current edge set $E$. At each
step we non-deterministically choose an edge $(\alpha, \beta)$ from $E$ and add it to $H$ (dotted edges in
steps 2 and 3). If the in-neighbours of $\beta$ in $H$ are now equal to its neighbours in $G_0$ (as in step
3), we move that in-star from $H$ to $G$ and add its out-edges to $E$ (\ruleName{$\sufficesE{}$-extend}, step
4). Otherwise, there is nothing more to do at this step and we continue with the remaining edges in $E$
(\ruleName{$\sufficesE{}$-pending}). The algorithm terminates when $E$ is empty
(\ruleName{$\sufficesE{}$-done}); any vertices still pending in $H$ (as in step 8) are vertices had only
partially sufficient inputs.

\begin{proposition}[$\sufficesAlg{}$ Computes $\sufficesR$]
   For any dependence graph $G$, any $X \subseteq \sources{G}$ and any $Y \subseteq \sinks{G}$ we have:
   \[X \sufficesAlg{G} Y \iff \sufficesR(X) = Y\]
\end{proposition}
