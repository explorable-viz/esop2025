\section{Conjugate Operators For Dynamic Dependence Graphs}
\label{sec:conjugate}
We introduce some preliminary notation (\secref{conjugate:notation}), before building up the intuition needed
to formally define \textit{demands} and \textit{demanded by} (\secref{conjugate:dependence-graphs}), and read
the algorithms that compute them (\secref{conjugate:dependence-graph-algos}).

\subsection{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:conjugate:notation}
We give some notation used in the rest of the paper. $X, Y$ range over finite sets.

\paragraph{Finite sequences}
Write to ${\seq{x}}$ denote a finite sequence of elements $\seqRange{x_1}{x_n}$. We also write $x \cons
\seq{x}$ for cons (prepend)  and $\seq{x} \cons x$ for snoc (append).

\paragraph{Finite maps}
Write $\set{\seq{\bind{k}{x}}}$ to denote a set of pairs $\seqRange{\bind{k_1}{x_1}}{\bind{k_n}{x_n}}$ where
keys $k_i$ are pairwise unique.

\paragraph{Disjoint union}
Write $X \disjunion Y$ to mean $X \cup Y$ where $X$ and $Y$ are disjoint. For finite maps $\phi, \psi$ write $\phi \disjunion \psi$ to mean $\phi \cup \psi$ where $\dom{\phi}$ and $\dom{\psi}$ are disjoint. Also write $x \cdot X$ or $X \cdot x$ to mean $X \disjunion \set{x}$.

\paragraph{Relative complement}
Write $X \setminus Y$ for the relative complement of $Y$ in $X$, i.e.~the set of elements of
$X$ that are not in $Y$.

\paragraph{Relational converse}
For a relation $R \subseteq X \times Y$, write $R^{-1} \subseteq Y \times X$ to denote the \emph{converse}
relation where $(y,x) \in R^{-1} \iff (x,y) \in R$.

\subsubsection{Boolean algebras}

A \emph{Boolean algebra} (or Boolean lattice) is a 6-tuple $X = (X, \meet, \join, \bot, \top, \neg)$ with
carrier $X$, distinguished elements bottom ($\bot$) and top ($\top$), commutative operations meet ($\meet$)
and join ($\join$) with $\top$ and $\bot$ as their respective units, and a unary operation negate ($\neg$)
satisfying $x \join \neg x = \top$ and $x \meet \neg x = \bot$. The operations $\meet$ and $\join$ distribute
over each other and induce a partial order $\leq$ over $X$.

Often we will be considering a specific finite Boolean algebra, namely the powerset $\powerset{X}$ whose
carrier is the set of subsets $X' \subseteq X$ ordered by set inclusion $\subseteq$. Bottom is the empty set
$\emptyset$, top is the whole set $X$, meet and join are given by $\cap$ and $\cup$ and negation by relative
complement, so that $\neg X' = X \setminus X'$.

\subsection{Dynamic Dependence Graphs}
\label{sec:conjugate:dependence-graphs}
We explain how we arrive at specific dependence graphs until \secref{core}, and here just introduce the basic
notion. A \emph{dynamic dependence graph}~\cite{agrawal90} is a directed graph $G = (V,E)$ with a set $V$ of
\emph{nodes} (or \emph{vertices}) and a set $E \subseteq V \times V$ of edges. In the dependence graph for a
particular program, nodes $\alpha \in V$ represents parts of values (either supplied to a program as inputs or
produced during execution) and edges $(\alpha, \beta) \in E$ indicate that, in the evaluation of the program,
the partial value associated to $\beta$ \textit{depends on} on the partial value associated to $\alpha$. This
diverges somewhat from traditional approaches to dynamic dependence graphs in only considering one type of
edge (data dependency) rather than separate data and control dependencies; moreover our edges point in the
direction of dependent nodes. (In the literature the other direction is somewhat more common.)

\begin{definition}[In-edges and out-edges]
For a graph $G=(V,E)$ and vertex $\alpha \in V$, write $\inE{G}(\alpha)$ for the in-edges of $\alpha$ in $G$
and $\outE{G}(\alpha)$ for its out-edges.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Sources and sinks]
For a graph $G = (V,E)$, write $\sources{G}$ for the \emph{sources} of $G$ (i.e.~those vertices with no
in-edges) and $\sinks{G}$ for the \emph{sinks} of $G$ (those with no out-edges).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Reachability relation]
Define the \emph{reachability relation} $R_G$ for a graph $G=(V,E)$ to be the reflexive transitive closure of
$E$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Opposite graph]
For a graph $G$ define the \emph{opposite} graph $\opGraph{G} \eqdef (V,E^{-1})$, where $\param^{-1}$ denotes
relational converse.
\end{definition}

\noindent (Taking the opposite graph has the effect of swapping sources and sinks.)

We can think of the reachability relation $R$ between the sources and sinks of $G$ as constituting an ``IO''
dependency relation for $G$, specifying how specific ``inputs'' (sources) are demanded by specific ``output''
(sinks). (So that $(x, y) \in R$ can be read as ``$x$ is \textit{demanded by} $y$''.) Since we are
actually interested in the relationship between input and outputs \emph{selections} (i.e.~sets of inputs and
outputs), we lift this IO relation to sets via the \textit{image} and \textit{preimage functions} of an
arbitrary relation, which here we denote using $\demandByR$ and $\demandR$:
\begin{definition}[Image and Preimage of a Relation]
   For a relation $R \subseteq X \times Y$, define $\demandByR_R: \powerset{X} \to \powerset{Y}$ and
   $\demandR_R : \powerset{Y} \to \powerset{X}$ as:
   \begin{itemize}
      \item $\demandByR_{R}(X') \eqdef \set{y \in Y \mid \exists x \in X'.(x,y) \in R}$ \hfill (image)
      \item $\demandR_{R}(Y') \eqdef \set{x \in X \mid \exists y \in Y'. (x,y) \in R}$ \hfill (preimage)
   \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\noindent When thinking of $R$ as a dependency relation, we can view the preimage $\demandR_{R}(Y')$ as the
set of inputs demanded by the elements of $Y'$, and the image $\demandByR_{R}(X')$ as the set of outputs that
the elements of $X'$ are demanded by. A trivial but important observation is that the image function for $R$
is the preimage function for its converse, i.e.~$\demandByR_{R} = \demandR_{R^{-1}}$.

\cd{improve motivation for sufficiency?}
A related question we may come across in data analysis tasks is that of \textit{sufficiency}. For the values
in $X'$ to be sufficient for some output, $y \in Y$, we need $x_i \in X'$ for every $(x_i, y) \in D$. As a
simple example, consider the expression $3 + 4$, neither of the subexpressions $3$ or $4$ are sufficient to
produce the result of evaluating the expression on their own. Notably, the set of outputs that a set of inputs
is necessary for is a superset of the outputs it is sufficient for.

We observe that the image and preimage of a relation form  a
\textit{conjugate pair}, in the sense of \citet{jonsson51}:

\begin{definition}[Conjugate Functions]
   For Boolean algebras $X$, $Y$ and functions $f: X \to Y$ and $g: Y \to X$, we say that $f, g$ form a
   \textit{conjugate pair} iff:
   $$
   f(x) \meet y \neq \bot \iff x \meet g(y) \neq \bot
   $$
\end{definition}

\noindent (\citeauthor{jonsson51} prefer a contrapositive formulation but we find this to be more intuitive.)

\begin{lemma}[Preimage and Image of a relation are conjugate]\label{lem:im-preim} For a relation $D \subseteq
   X \times Y$, $\demandByR_{R}$ and $\demandR_{R}$ are conjugate.
\end{lemma}

This makes sense as a condition for  \textit{demands} and
\textit{demanded by}: for some subsets $X',Y'$, if the output values
$X'$ is demanded by has an empty intersection with $Y'$, then we would expect the set
of input values that $Y'$ demands to have an empty intersection with $X'$.


We note that these two functions do not always form a Galois connection, since the composition of the two
functions is not necessarily inflationary, contradicting a basic fact of Galois connections, which we picture
in \figref{relation-not-galois}. In \figref{relation-not-galois-1}, we apply $\demandByR_R$ to $\set{x_1,
x_2}$, and find $\set{y_1, y_2}$. We then apply $\demandR$ to $\set{y_1, y_2}$, and get $\set{x_2, x_3}$,
which is neither a superset, nor a subset of the originally selected set. Since the composite function
$\demandR_R \after \demandByR_R$ is neither increasing or decreasing, the functions cannot form a Galois
connection. We picture the same scenario reversed in \figref{relation-not-galois-2}. We can read the green set
as unused inputs, or free outputs. The light blue sets are the elements we have "discovered" via applying the
two functions one after another. We can think of the darker blue sets as the values about which we discover
nothing new when applying the two functions.

\input{fig/conjugate/relations-not-galois}

We observe that if we restrict the set of inputs $X$ and outputs $Y$ in the graph such that there are
no inputs which are unused, then $\demandR$ will preserve $\bot$, i.e.~$\demandR(\emptyset) = \emptyset$.
This means that applying $\demandByR$ after $\demandR$, we will not lose any of
the originally selected inputs.
\begin{lemma}
   \item If $\demandByR_R(\emptyset) = \emptyset$, then $\demandR_R \after \demandByR_R$ is inflationary.
   \item If $\demandR_R(\emptyset) = \emptyset$, then $\demandByR_R \after \demandR_R$ is inflationary.
\end{lemma}
Similarly, if we have no outputs which are free, then $\demandByR$ will also
preserve $\bot$, and composing the functions in the opposite way will also
be increasing.

As an easy corollary, if $\demandR$ and $\demandByR$ preserve $\emptyset$, then they actually do form a Galois
connection, albeit an antitone one. This is useful to know, since we may be working in a context where we wish
to consider a filtered view of the data, where the user is shown only the data used by the original program.

\subsection{Computing Demands and Demanded By over Dependence Graphs}
\label{sec:conjugate:dependence-graph-algos}
We now show how to compute $\demandByR_{R_G}$ and $\demandR_{R_G}$ for the reachability relation $R_G$ of a
dependence graph $G$. Whilst one can view a graph as a collection of vertices and edges, we give an
alternative definition as a finite map $\set{\seq{\bind{v}{U}}}$, where $\bind{v}{U}$ denotes that a vertex
$v$ has outgoing edges to all vertices in $U \subseteq V$. In the rest of the paper, we use the two graph
definitions interchangeably, by picking the one that simplifies the formalisation.

\begin{definition}[Graphs as finite maps]
   A graph $G =(V,E)$ is a finite map $G_M \eqdef \set{\seq{\bind{v}{U}}}$,
   where $\bind{v}{U} \in G_M \iff v \in V$, $U \subseteq V$ and $(v, u_i) \in E$ for all $u_i \in U$
\end{definition}
%When constructing a graph incrementally, we may exploit this notation, and use
%$\bind{v}{U}$ to refer to a "star" graph, with one central node pointing at its
%neighbours.
%We will often refer to a graph without explicitly referring to its representation
%as a map, or a pair of sets.

Once a graph has been constructed, the set of nodes that a value is demanded by can be represented by the set
of nodes that are reachable by traversing the edges of the graph. Conversely, the set of nodes that a node
demands is represented by the nodes reachable from this node when traversing the edges of the graph in the
opposite direction, i.e. making use of the preimage of reachability. We use this intuition when defining
\textit{demands} and \textit{demanded by} over the dependence graph $G$:
%in terms of $R_G$, we refer to the dependence $G$:

\begin{definition}[Demands and Demanded By on a graph]
   For a graph $G = (V,E)$ with reachability relation $R_G \subseteq V \times V$, and the restriction $R'_G \eqdef R_G|_{\sources{G} \times \sinks{G}}$
   of $R_G$ to the sources and sinks of $G$, we have:
   \begin{itemize}
      \item $\demandR_G(X) \eqdef \demandR_{R'_G} (X)$ \hfill (demands)
      \item $\demandByR_G(Y) \eqdef \demandByR_{R'_G} (Y)$ \hfill (demanded by)
   \end{itemize}

\end{definition}

In \secref{overview}, we introduced the notions of related inputs and related outputs.
Now, we can provide a formal account for them.
\begin{definition}[Related Inputs and Outputs]
   For $G = (V,E)$, define:
   \begin{enumerate}
      \item $\relInput_{G} \eqdef \demandR_{G} \after \demandByR_{G}$\hfill (related inputs)
      \item $\relOutput_{G} \eqdef \demandByR_{G} \after \demandR_{G}$\hfill (related outputs)
   \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}
A related inputs query can now be phrased as "what else can reach the nodes I can
reach in the graph?", and a related outputs query can be phrased as "what else can
be reached by the things that reach me?".
To calculate a related inputs query, the first step is to select
the input of interest, $X' \subseteq \sources{G}$. We proceed by finding all nodes
in the output that our selected input nodes are demanded by, $\demandByR_{G}(X')$.
Then, we compute all the input nodes that the output nodes demand, i.e. $\demandR_{G}(\demandByR_{G}(X'))$.
Conversely, we calculate a related outputs query by first selecting
the output of interest, $Y' \subseteq \sinks{G}$. Then, we compute all nodes
in the input that our selected output nodes demand, $\demandR_{G}(Y')$, and, lastly,
all the output nodes that the input nodes $\demandR_{G}(Y')$ demand, $\demandByR_{G}(\demandR_{G}(Y'))$.


Intuitively, we can think of related inputs and related outputs as similar
to the notion of "cognacy" in natural languages: for related outputs we are finding nodes with
common ancestors, and for related inputs we are finding nodes with common descendants.


We compute demandedBy using the algorithm $\demandsAlg{}$ detailed in \figref{conjugate:algorithms:demands}.
The algorithm computes reachability by incrementally building a subgraph $G'$,
consisting of all nodes and edges that can be traversed from the initial starting set.
$\demandsAlg{}$ begins by taking a set of nodes and accumulating their neighbours.
Every time a new node is reached by traversing an edge, we enqueue its own
neighbours, and add it to the graph we are constructing (\ruleName{$\demandsVert{}$-extend}).
If we traverse an edge and reach a node we've already met, we simply skip it
(\ruleName{$\demandsVert{}$-skip})
At the algorithm's termination (\ruleName{$\demandsVert{}$-done}), we have traversed every
edge and node reachable from the input set.
Once it has terminated, and we have constructed the subgraph of demanded nodes,
we filter out any nodes which do not appear as outputs by only considering the
graph's set of sinks, $\sinks{G'}$ (\ruleName{$\demandsAlg{}$}).

\begin{proposition}[The reaches relation connects sources to sinks]

\begin{proposition}[The ReachesV relation implies subgraphing]
  For all graphs $G$, $G'$ and $G_0$ and vertex set $V$ then:

      G, V \demandsVert{G_0} G'
      \implies
      G \subseteq G' \subseteq G_0

\subsection{Relationship to Galois Slicing}

We mentioned earlier that $\demandByR_R(X')$ can be read as asking "What is $X'$
necessary for?" Asking this question naturally leads us to ask how we can
adapt this analyis to answer the question "what is $X'$ \textit{sufficient} for?"
In fact, we can use the notion of sufficiency as a test of correctness. If we
begin by selecting an output of interest, we would expect the data it
demands to also be sufficient to reconstruct it. This leads us
to define the following \textit{sufficiency} analysis.

\begin{definition}[Sufficiency]
   For a graph $G=(V,E)$ with inputs $X \subseteq V$, outputs $Y \subseteq V$ we define,
   and a subset of the inputs $X' \subseteq X$
   \begin{itemize}
      \item $\sufficientR_G(X') \eqdef \set{y \in Y \mid \demandR_G (y) \subseteq X' }$ \hfill (sufficiency)
   \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

This definition looks remarkably similar to $\demandByR_R$, albeit the definition of $\sufficientR_R$ needs
every ancestor to be in the input set, rather than any ancestor to be in the input set. We can, in fact, turn
this query on sufficiency into $\demandByR_R$, using the De Morgan Dual.

\begin{definition}[De Morgan Dual]
   For Boolean algebras $A, B$, and a function $f: A \to B$, we define the
   \textit{De Morgan Dual} $\dual{f}: A \to B$ of $f$ as:
   $$
   \dual{f} \eqdef \neg_B \after f \after \neg_A
   $$
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Correspondence Between Conjugates and Galois Connections]
   Let $A,B$ be Boolean algebras, $f: A \to B$ and $g: B \to A$.
   The following statements are equivalent:
   \begin{itemize}
      \item $f$ and $g$ form a conjugate pair
      \item $f$ and $\dual{g}$ form a Galois connection
   \end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[Sufficiency is the dual of necessity]
   For a graph $G = (V,E)$ with inputs $X \subseteq V$ and a subset of the inputs $X' \subseteq X$
   the following holds:
   $$\dual{\demandByR_G(X')} = \sufficientR_G(X')$$
\end{lemma}

Because of the lemma above, we could reuse $\demandsAlg{}$ in order to compute
this, by taking the complements of the input sets, running $\demandsAlg{}$, and
then complementing the output, however this could in practise be inefficient,
since taking complements of large sets can be an expensive operation. We therefore
provide a more efficient implementation of the sufficiency analyis, detailed in
\figref{conjugate:slicing:forward}.

The algorithm $\fwdAlg{G}$ builds a subgraph of the original dependence graph,
similar to $\demandsAlg{}$. In the main subroutine, $\fwdEdges{}$, we consider
the subgraph under construction $G$, the subgraph of pending nodes $H$, and
the current set of edges under consideration $E$. For efficiency, the algorithm
proceeds in a manner which allows it to only traverse each edge in the graph once.
When considering an edge $(\alpha, \beta)$ in the edge list, $E$, we proceed by
adding it to $H$.
If doing so causes the neighbours of $\alpha$ in $H$ to be equal
to its neighbours in $G_0$ (the base dependence graph), then we can remove $\alpha$ from $H$, and add it
to $G$ (\ruleName{$\fwdEdges{}$-extend}), before adding its outgoing edges to $E$.

Because the edges in the parent graph $G$ all point in the direction of inputs to
outputs, computing the inward neighbours of a node is inefficient in the adjacency
map. We therefore perform the check related to input neighbors of a node on the
opposite adjacency map.

Otherwise, we extend the edges in $H$ with $(\alpha, \beta)$, and proceed (\ruleName{$\fwdEdges{}$-pending}).
The algorithm terminates when there are no more edges to be considered (\ruleName{$\fwdEdges{}$-pending}).
When this happens, it is possible that $H$ is non-empty. This implies that during
the algorithm's execution, we have traversed at least one node without traversing
all of its in-edges. Thus, $H$ forms a sort of front of nodes for which the
input nodes only provide partial information.

\begin{proposition}[Forwards Computes Sufficency]
   For a dependence graph $G= (V,E)$, and a subset of the inputs $X' \subseteq \sources{G} \subset V$
   the following holds:
   $$
       X' \fwdAlg{G} \sinks{G'} \iff \sufficientR(X') = \sinks{G'}
   $$
\end{proposition}
