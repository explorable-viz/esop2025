\section{Conjugate Operators For Dynamic Dependence Graphs}
\label{sec:conjugate}

In this section we introduce Boolean algebras and other preliminaries (\secref{conjugate:notation}); dynamic
dependence graphs (\secref{conjugate:dependence-graphs}), and the image and preimage functions for a relation
and their De Morgan duals (\secref{conjugate:image-preimage}). We then extend these operators to dependence
graphs (\secref{conjugate:dependence-graph-functions}) via their IO relation, and give algorithms for computing
them over a given graph (\secref{conjugate:dependence-graph-algos}).

\subsection{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:conjugate:notation}
We give some notation used in the rest of the paper. $X, Y$ range over finite sets.

\paragraph{Finite sequences}
Write to ${\seq{x}}$ denote a finite sequence of elements $\seqRange{x_1}{x_n}$, with $\seqEmpty$ as the empty sequence. Concatenation of two sequences $\seq{x}$ and $\seq{x}'$ is written $\seq{x} \concat \seq{x}'$.
We also write $x \cons \seq{x}$ for cons (prepend) and $\seq{x} \cons x$ for snoc (append).

\paragraph{Finite maps}
Write $\set{\seq{\bind{k}{x}}}$ to denote a set of pairs $\seqRange{\bind{k_1}{x_1}}{\bind{k_n}{x_n}}$ where
keys $k_i$ are pairwise unique.

\paragraph{Disjoint union}
Write $X \disjunion Y$ to mean $X \cup Y$ where $X$ and $Y$ are disjoint, and also write $x \cdot X$ or $X \cdot x$ to mean $X \disjunion \set{x}$. For finite maps $\phi, \psi$ write $\phi \disjunion \psi$ to mean $\phi \cup \psi$ where $\dom{\phi}$ and $\dom{\psi}$ are disjoint.

\paragraph{Relative complement}
Write $X \setminus Y$ for the relative complement of $Y$ in $X$, i.e.~the set of elements of
$X$ that are not in $Y$.

\paragraph{Relational converse}
Write ${\param}^{-1}$ for the involutive operation that takes a relation $R \subseteq X \times Y$ to its
\emph{converse} $R^{-1} \subseteq Y \times X$ where $(y,x) \in R^{-1} \iff (x,y) \in R$.

\subsubsection{Boolean algebras}

A \emph{Boolean algebra} (or Boolean lattice) is a 6-tuple $A = (A, \meet, \join, \bot, \top, \neg)$ with
carrier $A$, distinguished elements bottom ($\bot$) and top ($\top$), commutative operations meet ($\meet$)
and join ($\join$) with $\top$ and $\bot$ as respective units, and a unary operation negate ($\neg$)
satisfying $x \join \neg x = \top$ and $x \meet \neg x = \bot$. The operations $\meet$ and $\join$ distribute
over each other and induce a partial order $\leq$ over $X$.

Often we will be considering a specific finite Boolean algebra, namely the powerset $\powerset{X}$ whose
carrier is the set of subsets $X' \subseteq X$ ordered by inclusion $\subseteq$. Bottom is the empty set
$\emptyset$, top is the whole set $X$, meet and join are given by $\cap$ and $\cup$ and negation by relative
complement, so that $\neg X' = X \setminus X'$.

\subsection{Dynamic Dependence Graphs}
\label{sec:conjugate:dependence-graphs}

We explain how we arrive at specific dependence graphs in \secref{core}, and here just introduce the basic
notion. A \emph{dynamic dependence graph}~\cite{agrawal90} is a directed acyclic graph $G = (V,E)$ with a set
$V$ of \emph{vertices} and set $E \subseteq V \times V$ of edges. In the dependence graph for a particular
program, vertices $\alpha, \beta \in V$ represents \emph{parts} of values (either supplied to the program as
inputs or produced during evaluation) and edges $(\alpha, \beta) \in E$ indicate that, in the evaluation of
the program, the partial value associated to $\beta$ \textit{depends on} on the partial value associated to
$\alpha$. (The notion of a ``part'' of a value will be made precise in \secref{core}.) This diverges
somewhat from traditional approaches to dynamic dependence graphs in only considering one type of edge (data
dependency) rather than separate data and control dependencies; moreover our edges point in the direction of
dependent vertices, whereas in the literature the other direction is somewhat more common.

\begin{definition}[In-edges and out-edges]
For a graph $G=(V,E)$ and vertex $\alpha \in V$, write $\inE{G}(\alpha)$ for the in-edges of $\alpha$ in $G$
and $\outE{G}(\alpha)$ for its out-edges.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Sources and sinks]
For a graph $G = (V,E)$, write $\sources{G}$ for the \emph{sources} of $G$ (i.e.~those vertices with no
in-edges) and $\sinks{G}$ for the \emph{sinks} of $G$ (those with no out-edges).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Reachability relation]
Define the \emph{reachability relation} for a graph $G=(V,E)$ to be the reflexive transitive closure of $E$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Opposite graph]
For a graph $G$ define the \emph{opposite} graph $\opGraph{G} \eqdef (V,E^{-1})$ where $\param^{-1}$ denotes
relational converse.
\end{definition}

\noindent (Taking the opposite graph has the effect of swapping sources and sinks.)

\subsubsection{IO Relation for a Graph}

Reachability in $G$ induces a relation just between sources and sinks, which we call the \emph{IO} relation of
$G$.

\begin{definition}[IO relation]
For any dependence graph $G$ with reachability relation $R$, define the \emph{IO} relation of $G$ to be $R \cap (\sources{G} \times \sinks{G})$.
\end{definition}

The IO relation specifies how specific inputs (sources) are demanded by specific outputs (sinks); thus we will
interpret $(x, y) \in R$ as ``$x$ is \textit{demanded by} $y$''. Clearly the IO relation of $G$ is the
converse of the IO relation of $\opGraph{G}$. Since we are in fact interested in the relationship between
input and outputs \emph{selections} (namely sets of inputs and outputs), we now consider how such a relation
$R \subseteq X \times Y$ lifts to $\powerset{X}$ and $\powerset{Y}$ via the \textit{image} and
\textit{preimage} functions for $R$, which we will denote using $\demandedByR$ and $\demandsR$.

\subsection{Conjugate Image and Preimage Functions}
\label{sec:conjugate:image-preimage}

\begin{definition}[Image and Preimage Functions for a Relation]
   For a relation $R \subseteq X \times Y$, define $\demandedByR_R: \powerset{X} \to \powerset{Y}$ and
   $\demandsR_R : \powerset{Y} \to \powerset{X}$ as:
   \begin{enumerate}
      \item $\demandedByR_{R}(X') \eqdef \set{y \in Y \mid \exists x \in X'.(x,y) \in R}$ \hfill (image)
      \item $\demandsR_{R}(Y') \eqdef \set{x \in X \mid \exists y \in Y'. (x,y) \in R}$ \hfill (preimage)
   \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\noindent Clearly the image function for $R$ is the preimage function for its converse; this is important enough to state as a lemma:

\begin{lemma}[Image as Preimage of Converse Relation]
\label{lem:conjugate:image-preimage-converse}
$\demandedByR_{R} = \demandsR_{R^{-1}}$
\end{lemma}

When thinking of $R$ as an IO relation, the image $\demandedByR_{R}(X')$ consists of all outputs that any
element of $X'$ is demanded by, and the preimage $\demandsR_{R}(Y')$ consists of all inputs demanded by any
element of $Y'$. If $R$ happens to be a function, the image and preimage functions form a \emph{Galois
connection} (\defref{conjugate:galois-connection} below); for a proper relation they form a \textit{conjugate
pair}, in the sense of \citet{jonsson51}:

%Notably, the set of outputs that a set of inputs is necessary for is a superset of the outputs it is
%sufficient for.

\begin{definition}[Conjugate Functions]
   For Boolean algebras $A$, $B$, functions $f: A \to B$ and $g: B \to A$ form a \textit{conjugate pair} iff:
   $$
   f(x) \meet y = \bot \iff x \meet g(y) = \bot
   $$
\end{definition}

\noindent (\citeauthor{jonsson51} consider only endofunctions; here we extend the idea of conjugacy to maps
between Boolean algebras.)

\begin{lemma}[Preimage and Image of a relation are conjugate]
For any relation $R \subseteq X \times Y$ the functions $\demandedByR_{R}$ and $\demandsR_{R}$ are conjugate.
\end{lemma}

This should be intuitive enough: for any subsets $X' \subseteq X$ and $Y' \subseteq Y$, if the elements ``on
the right'' to which $X'$ is related are disjoint from $Y'$, then there are no edges in $R$ from $X'$ to $Y'$;
and in virtue of that, the elements ``on the left'' to which $Y'$ is related must also be disjoint from $X'$.

\subsubsection{Dual Image and Preimage Functions}

If $R$ is an IO relation, then $\demandedByR_R(X)$ effectively asks ``what is $X$ necessary for?'' This
naturally leads to the question ``what is $X$ \emph{sufficient} for?'' In fact, we can use the notion of
sufficiency as a test of correctness. If we begin by selecting an output of interest, we would expect the data
it demands to also be sufficient to reconstruct it. This leads us to define the following pair of functions,
which also form a conjugate pair.

\begin{definition}[Dual Image and Preimage Functions for a Relation]
   For any relation $R \subseteq X \times Y$, define $\sufficesR_R: \powerset{X} \to \powerset{Y}$ and $\preimageDualR_R: \powerset{Y} \to \powerset{X}$ as the composition:
   \begin{enumerate}
      \item $\sufficesR_R(X') \eqdef \set{y \in Y \mid \nexists x \in X \setminus X'.(x, y) \in R}$ \hfill (dual image)
      \item $\preimageDualR_R(Y') \eqdef \set{x \in X \mid \nexists y \in Y \setminus Y'.(x, y) \in R}$ \hfill (dual preimage)
   \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

This definition is similar to $\demandedByR_R$ except that $\sufficesR_R$ needs every ancestor to be in the
input set, rather than any ancestor to be in the input set. We can, in fact, turn this query on sufficiency
into $\demandedByR_R$, using the De Morgan Dual.

\begin{definition}[De Morgan Dual]
   \label{def:conjugate:de-morgan-dual}
   Suppose a function $f: A \to B$ between Boolean algebras with $\neg_A$ and $\neg_B$ the negation operators
   of $A$ and $B$. Define the \textit{De Morgan Dual} $\dual{f}: A \to B$ of $f$ as:
   \[ \dual{f} \eqdef \neg_B \after f \after \neg_A \]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Galois connection]
\label{def:conjugate:galois-connection}
Suppose $A, B$ are partial orders. Then monotone functions $f: A \to B$ and $g: B \to A$ form a \textit{Galois
connection} iff
\[ f(x) \leq y \iff x \leq g(y) \]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Correspondence Between Conjugates and Galois Connections]
   Suppose functions $f: A \to B$ and $g: B \to A$ between Boolean algebras. The following statements are
   equivalent:
   \begin{enumerate}
      \item $f$ and $g$ form a conjugate pair
      \item $f$ and $\dual{g}$ form a Galois connection
   \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[Duality of image and preimage functions]
   \label{lem:conjugate:image-preimage-duality}
   \item
   \begin{enumerate}
      \item $\dual{\demandedByR_R} = \sufficesR_R$
      \item $\dual{\demandsR_R} = \preimageDualR_R$
   \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\subsection{$\demandsR$ and $\demandedByR$ over Dependence Graphs}
\label{sec:conjugate:dependence-graph-functions}

We now extend the notion of $\demandsR$ and $\demandedByR$ to a dependence graph $G$ via its IO
relation. The set of outputs \emph{demanded by} some inputs $X$ is simply the subset of the outputs of $G$
reachable from $X$, i.e.~the intersection of $\demandedByR_{R}(X)$ with $\sinks{G}$. Conversely, the set of
inputs that some outputs $Y$ \emph{demands} is simply the subset of the inputs of $G$ reachable from $Y$ by
traversing the graph in the opposite direction, i.e.~the intersection of $\demandsR_{R}$ with $\sources{G}$.

\begin{definition}[$\demandsR$ and $\demandedByR$ for a dependence graph]
   For a graph $G$ with IO relation $R$, define:
   \begin{enumerate}
      \item $\demandedByR_G \eqdef \demandedByR_{R}: \powerset{\sources{G}} \to \powerset{\sinks{G}}$ \hfill (demanded by)
      \item $\demandsR_G \eqdef \demandsR_{R}: \powerset{\sinks{G}} \to \powerset{\sources{G}}$ \hfill (demands)
   \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Computing $\demandsR_G$ and $\demandedByR_G$ and their duals}
\label{sec:conjugate:dependence-graph-algos}

We now show how to compute $\demandedByR_{G}$ and $\demandsR_{G}$ and their duals $\sufficesR_{G}$ and
$\preimageDualR_{G}$ for a dependence graph $G$. One advantage of graphs over the trace-based approach of
\citet{perera22} is that a single algorithm can be readily adapted to compute all 4 operators; given a
procedure for $\demandedByR_{G}$, for example, we can compute its conjugate $\demandsR_{G}$ as
$\demandedByR_{\opGraph{G}}$ (by \lemref{conjugate:image-preimage-converse}) and its dual $\sufficesR_{G}$ by
pre- and post-composing with negation (\defref{conjugate:de-morgan-dual}).

For efficiency, however, we give a direct procedure both for $\demandedByR_{G}$
(\secref{conjugate:algorithm:demandedBy}) and $\sufficesR_{G}$ (\secref{conjugate:algorithm:suffices}), allowing
us to compute the other two operators via the opposite graph; this is in some situations more efficient than
relying on the De Morgan dual, which can cause more graph to be traversed than would be otherwise. Both
procedures factor through an auxiliary operation that computes a ``slice'' of the original graph, i.e.~a
contiguous subgraph. While it is technically possible to compute the desired image/preimage of the IO relation
without creating this intermediate graph, we anticipate many use cases which will make use of the graph slice;
these are discussed in more detail in \secref{conclusion}. Our implementation makes it easy to flip between
$G$ and $\opGraph{G}$ so we freely make use of both in the algorithms to access out-neighbours and
in-neighbours.

An alternative presentation of graphs in terms of adjacency maps is useful notationally.

\begin{definition}[Graph as adjacency map]
   A graph $G = (V,E)$ can also be specified as an \emph{adjacency map} $G: V \to \powerset{V}$ from vertices
   to sets of out neighbours, where
   \[G(\alpha) = V' \iff \alpha \in V\text{ and }\forall\beta \in V'.(\alpha, \beta) \in E\]
\end{definition}

\subsubsection{Direct algorithm for $\demandedByR_{G}$ (\emph{demanded by})}
\label{sec:conjugate:algorithm:demandedBy}

\begin{definition}[$\demandedByAlg{G}$]
\label{def:conjugate:algorithm:demandedBy}
\figref{conjugate:algorithms:demands} defines the family of relations $\demandedByAlg{G}$ for dependence graph $G$.
\end{definition}

For a dependence graph $G$ and inputs $X \subseteq \sources{G}$, the judgement $X\; \smash{\demandedByAlg{G}}
Y$ says that $X$ is demanded by $Y \subseteq \sinks{G}$. It is possible to read an algorithm directly from the
inductive definition, treating it as a function with $V$ and index $G$ as inputs.\footnote{Any choice between
\ruleName{skip} and \ruleName{extend} is made deterministic by the disjointness of $G$ and $H$ in
\ruleName{extend} implying the opposite side condition to \ruleName{skip} ($\alpha \not\in \V(G)$), and with
termination implied by eventual saturation of the first argument by all of the edges in the indexing graph
leading from the vertices in second argument, after which a finite sequence of \ruleName{skip} can be applied
to reach the base case of \ruleName{done}.} The auxiliary operation $G \demandedByV{G}$ maintains a set of
vertices still to process and builds the graph slice $G' \subseteq G$ consisting of all vertices reachable
from the initial set and all edges between them. If $X$ is empty (\ruleName{done}) the graph slice $G'$ is
complete and can be returned. Otherwise we select an arbitrary vertex $\alpha$ from $X$. If $\alpha$ is
already in $G'$ (\ruleName{skip}), there is nothing to do at this step and we continue with the remaining
vertices. Otherwise, we add the out-star at $\alpha$ to $G'$ and the out-neighbours to the pending vertices
(\ruleName{extend}). When $\demandedByV{}$ is done, $\demandedByAlg{}$ extracts the output set from the graph
slice by intersecting with $\sinks{G}$.

   \[X \demandedByAlg{G} Y \iff \demandedByR_G(X) = Y\]
\end{proposition}

\subsubsection{Direct algorithm for $\sufficesR_{G}$ (\emph{suffices})}
\label{sec:conjugate:algorithm:suffices}

\begin{definition}[$\sufficesAlg{G}$]
\label{def:conjugate:algorithm:suffices}
\figref{conjugate:slicing:forward} defines the family of relations $\sufficesAlg{G}$ for dependence graph $G$.
\end{definition}

For a dependence graph $G$, the algorithm $\sufficesAlg{}$ also delegates to an auxiliary procedure
$\sufficesE{}$ which builds a graph slice $G$', similar to $\demandsAlg{}$ except that the direction of the
slice is inverted, i.e.~$G' \subseteq \opGraph{G}$. The judgement $\sufficesE{}$ takes the graph slice under
construction $G'$, a ``pending'' subgraph $H \subseteq G$ with $\sources{H} \subseteq \sinks{G'}$, and the
current set of edges under consideration $E$. For efficiency, the algorithm traverses each edge in the graph
at most once.

\figref{suffices-algo-diagram} shows an example run of the algorithm. Green vertices have been included into
$G'$ because all of their in-edges in $G$ are out-edges in $G'$; orange vertices are ``pending'', with at
least one in-edge from $G$ as an out-edge in $H$; blue vertices are unvisited. Dashed edges indicate the
current edge set $E$. At each step we non-deterministically choose an edge $(\alpha, \beta)$ from $E$ and add
the opposite edge $(\beta, \alpha)$ to $H$ (dotted edges in steps 2 and 3). If the out-neighbours of $\beta$
in $H$ are now equal to its in-neighbours in $G$ (as in step 3), we move that out-star from $H$ to $G$ and add
the in-edges of $\beta$ in $G$ to $E$ (\ruleName{$\sufficesE{}$-extend}, step 4). Otherwise, there is nothing
more to do at this step and we continue with the remaining edges in $E$ (\ruleName{$\sufficesE{}$-pending}).
The algorithm terminates when $E$ is empty (\ruleName{$\sufficesE{}$-done}); any vertices still pending in $H$
(as in step 8) are vertices had only partially sufficient inputs.

\input{fig/conjugate/suffices-algo-explanation}

\begin{proposition}[$\sufficesAlg{}$ Computes $\sufficesR$]
   \label{prop:sufficesAlg-computes-suffices}
   For any dependence graph $G$, any $X \subseteq \sources{G}$ and any $Y \subseteq \sinks{G}$ we have:
   \[X \sufficesAlg{G} Y \iff \sufficesR(X) = Y\]
\end{proposition}

\subsubsection{Alternative algorithm for $\demandedByR_{G}$}
\label{sec:conjugate:algorithm:demandedBy-suff-dual}

\begin{definition}[$\demandedBySuffDualAlg{G}$]
\label{def:conjugate:algorithm:demandedBy-alt}
Define the following family of relations $\demandedBySuffDualAlg{G}$ for any dependence graph $G$:
\[
\sources{G} \setminus X \demandedByAlg{G} Y
\iff
X \demandedBySuffDualAlg{G} \sinks{G} \setminus Y
\]
\end{definition}

The following is then an easy consequence of \propref{sufficesAlg-computes-suffices},
\lemref{conjugate:image-preimage-duality} and \defref{conjugate:de-morgan-dual}.

\begin{proposition}[$\demandedBySuffDualAlg{}$ Computes $\demandedByR$]
   For any dependence graph $G$, any $X \subseteq \sources{G}$ and any $Y \subseteq \sinks{G}$ we have:
   \[X \demandedBySuffDualAlg{G} Y \iff \demandedByR_G(X) = Y\]
\end{proposition}

\subsection{$\relInputR$ and $\relOutputR$ over dependence graphs}

Now we can provide a formal account of the notions of related inputs and related outputs introduced in
\secref{overview}.

\begin{definition}[Related Inputs and Outputs]
   For a dependence graph $G$ define:
   \begin{enumerate}
      \item $\relInputR_{G} \eqdef \demandsR_{G} \after \demandedByR_{G}$\hfill (related inputs)
      \item $\relOutputR_{G} \eqdef \demandedByR_{G} \after \demandsR_{G}$\hfill (related outputs)
   \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Intuitively, related outputs and related inputs are relations of \emph{cognacy} (common ancestry) in $G$ and
$\opGraph{G}$ respectively. For a set of inputs $X \subseteq \sources{G}$, related inputs asks ``what other
inputs are demanded by the outputs which demand X?'', and first finds all outputs $\demandedByR_{G}(X)$ that
our inputs are demanded by, and then computes the inputs that those outputs demand,
i.e.~$\demandsR_{G}(\demandedByR_{G}(X'))$. For a set of outputs $Y \subseteq \sinks{G}$, related outputs asks
``what other outputs demand the inputs that Y demands'', and first finds all inputs $\demandsR_{G}(Y')$ that
our outputs demand, and then computes the outputs that those inputs $\demandsR_{G}(Y)$ demand,
i.e.~$\demandedByR_{G}(\demandsR_{G}(Y))$.

Neither $\relInputR_{G}$ nor $\relOutputR_{G}$ is necessarily inflationary, as illustrated in
\figref{relation-not-galois}. In \figref{relation-not-galois-1}, the uppermost vertex on the left is part of
the initial input selection $X'$, but is not preserved by the $\demandsR_G \after \demandedByR_G$ round-trip
because it is not demanded by any output. In \figref{relation-not-galois-2}, the uppermost vertex on the right
is part of the initial output selection $Y'$, but is not preserved by the $\demandedByR_G \after \demandsR_G$
round-trip because it does not demand any input.

\input{fig/conjugate/relations-not-galois}

If we constrain things so that there are no inputs which are unused, then $\demandsR$ will preserve $\bot$,
i.e.~$\demandsR(\emptyset) = \emptyset$, and $\relOutputR_{G}$ will preserve the original output selection.
Similarly, if we constrain things so that all outputs demand some input, then $\demandedByR$ will preserve
$\bot$, and $\relInputR_{G}$ will preserve the original input selection:

\begin{lemma}
\item
\begin{enumerate}
   \item If $\demandedByR_G(\emptyset) = \emptyset$, then for all $X$ we have $\relInputR_{G}(X) \geq X$.
   \item If $\demandsR_G(\emptyset) = \emptyset$, then for all $Y$ we have $\relOutputR_{G}(Y) \geq Y$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

As an easy corollary, if $\demandsR_G$ and $\demandedByR_G$ both preserve $\emptyset$ then in fact they form
an antitone Galois connection. These observations have practical implications: for example it is sometimes
convenient to consider a filtered view of the data where by default the user only sees data which is actually
used somewhere, and in such a setup a related inputs query will never cause any selected data to become
unselected.
