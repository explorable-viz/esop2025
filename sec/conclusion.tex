\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Our work is motivated by the difficulty and time-consuming nature of producing interactive data visualisations
\todo{not according to the introduction -- our work is motived by the need for explorable, transparent
research outputs like data visualisations}. Visualisations provide a valuable mechanism for communicating
research and performing exploratory data analysis, but interactive visualisations \todo{there are all kinds of
interactivity, most of which aren't related to the kinds of provenance query we support} are often bespoke
solutions for a single application. By shifting the burden into a language runtime, time and effort can be
saved by the developer, who needs only to express their data analysis as a program creating the relevant
charts, and gets interaction functionality \todo{transparency} for free. However, prior systems that aimed at
integrating these features proved to be sensitive to changes in the programming language, and made use of
bidirectional analyses which were difficult to implement.

In this paper, we introduced a novel bidirectional program analysis framework that decomposes the system into
an evaluator that builds a dynamic dependence graph, and a pair of language agnostic graph algorithms. By
doing so, we achieve a separation of the analyses from the language, a simpler implementation of the analyses
themselves, and an overall improved performance for running~analyses.

The analyses in their current form are only sensitive to the \emph{extension} -- the IO relation -- of the
