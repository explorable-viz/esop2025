\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Visualisations are a valuable tool for communicating science and other data-driven claims, but are hard to
make sense of, trust and verify. Visualisations and other outputs that are ``transparent'' -- that can reveal
their relationship to data to an interested reader -- are more informative and trustworthy, but also difficult
to produce. In this paper, we introduced a novel bidirectional program analysis framework for a
general-purpose programming language that makes it easier to create transparent visualisations by shifting
much of the burden to the programming language runtime. Relative to prior work based on execution traces and
bidirectional interpreters, our approach also makes life easier for the language implementor, by decomposing
the system into a single evaluator that builds a dependence graph, and a pair of language-agnostic
bidirectional graph algorithms. We also showed an overall performance improvement for provenance queries, at
the cost of some overhead in building the dependence graph.
