\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Whether setting national policy or simply deciding what groceries to buy, when making decisions we
increasingly rely on charts, figures and text created by scientists and journalists. These sorts of visual and
textual summaries are essential to making and supporting claims, but are \emph{opaque} in the sense of being
unable to reveal anything how they relate to the data they were derived from, making them hard to fact-check
or drill into \emph{in situ}. If we know the problem domain well, then with access to the source code and data
sets we may be able to reverse engineer some of these relationships. But this is unlikely to be easy, and
often we have little choice but to take things on trust. These difficulties are further compounded when the
information presented draws on multiple data sources, such medical meta-analyses ~\citep{nakamura19}, ensemble
models~\citep{virdee23}, or queries that span multiple database tables~\citep{selinger79}.

With traditional print media, this sort of ``disconnect'' between chart and figures and the underlying data is
to be expected, but for digital media, other options are open to us. In this paper, we introduce a new program
analysis framework which supports interactive exploration of input-output relationships directly via visual
outputs such as charts or tables. While this idea is not entirely new (see for example \citet{psallidas18}
propose a deep relationship between data provenance and data visualisation), our framework supports a novel
form of provenance query which we call \emph{related inputs}, as well as lending itself to easier
implementation than prior work.

Related inputs characterises the relation of mutual relevance which arises between two input elements when
they contribute to a common output element. Users are able to ask questions of the form ``What outputs use
this data element, and what other data elements are used along with it?'' To answer such questions, we make
use of \emph{dynamic dependence graphs} (DDGs), an execution record used in the program slicing literature for
analysing imperative programs~\cite{ferrante87}. We formalise related inputs in terms of the composite
relation $R^{-1} \relcomp R$ where $R$ is the reachability relation for a dependence graph $G$, and give a
procedure for computing related inputs over $G$. Borrowing a term from linguistics, we say that inputs related
in this way are \emph{cognate} because their relatedness amounts to having a common ancestor in $G$. The key
mathematical idea is the concept of ``conjugate'' operators~\cite{jonsson51}, functions between Boolean
algebras which come in reciprocating pairs and which when ``self-composed'' calculate relations of cognacy.

Conjugate pairs are closely related to Galois connections: if $f$ and $g$ are conjugate, then $f$ and the De
Morgan dual of $g$ form a Galois connection. This relates our approach to earlier work based on Galois
connections and execution traces that addressed the problem of \emph{brushing and linking} in data
visualisation~\cite{perera22}. In our framework, the flipped composite $R \relcomp R^{-1}$ gives rise to a
dual cognacy relation between output features which compete for common input data; composing related outputs
with suitable projections at either end recovers the brushing and linking approach of the earlier work.
However, our language-agnostic graphical approach is easier to implement, and also fast enough to support the
kind of interactive scenarios we have in mind.

\subsection{Contributions}

The specific contributions of the paper are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item We motivate the novel cognacy operator $\relInput$ (``related inputs'') from an end-user perspective
(\secref{example}). Using a realistic example, we unpack the intuitive relationship between its two components
$\demandR$ (``demands'') and $\demandByR$ (``demanded by'') in terms of \citeauthor{jonsson51}'s notion of
conjugacy and also introduce the dual cognacy operator $\relOutput$ (``related outputs'').
\item We introduce the formal setting of conjugate operators between Boolean algebras (\secref{conjugate}). We
explain how the image and preimage functions for a relation $D \subseteq X \times Y$ form a conjugate pair
$\demandByR_{D}$ and $\demandR_{D}$ between the powersets of $X$ and $Y$ and that $f$ and $g$ being conjugate
is equivalent to $f$ and the De Morgan dual of $g$ forming a Galois connection. We give procedures for
computing $\demandR_{D}$ and $\demandByR_{D}$ over a directed graph with reachability relation $D$.
\item We define a core functional language with an operational semantics that pairs every result with a
dynamic dependence graph suitable for computing $\demandR$ and $\demandByR$ (\secref{core}). We show how to represent (parts) of values as nodes in the graph.
\item We compare the performance of our implementation based on graphs and conjugates with an implementation
due to \citet{perera22} based on traces and Galois connections (\secref{evaluation}). We contrast the overhead
of building trace vs.~building dependence graphs, computing $\demandR$ and $\demandByR$ over each, and the
relative implementation burden of the two approaches.
\end{itemize}

a discussion on some limitations of the present system and how it might be developed further in the future.
